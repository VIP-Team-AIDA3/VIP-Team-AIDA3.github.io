## Classical Guidance and Control

This subteam develops and characterizes the classical guidance and control stack that every other flight-facing project measures itself against. Work covers lateral guidance for waypoint and loiter tracking, longitudinal energy management for coupled speed and altitude commands, and the inner-loop attitude controllers that sit beneath them, along with the gain tuning and stability margins that make a controller trustworthy on a real airframe rather than only in simulation. Because a learned policy is only interesting if it beats a well-tuned conventional one, a central deliverable is a documented, reproducible baseline: the same mission profiles flown with classical guidance under the same wind, turbulence, and sensor-noise conditions used by the reinforcement learning subteam, with tracking error, control effort, and failure modes reported in a form the two teams can compare directly. The subteam also inherits the trajectory-generation problem of producing flyable paths that respect turn-rate and climb-rate limits, which connects it to the airspace geometry work upstream.

Expected activities include: 1) Literature review on classical fixed-wing guidance laws and autopilot architectures 2) Reconstruction and documentation of the results carried over from spring 3) Implementation of lateral and longitudinal guidance laws in the competition simulator 4) Systematic gain tuning and stability margin analysis against the SysID models 5) Definition of a shared benchmark suite of mission profiles and disturbance conditions 6) Head-to-head evaluation against the learned path-planner 7) Hardware-in-the-loop testing and flight validation at PURT.

Team members will work in Python and the competition simulator alongside the vehicle autopilot configuration. This subteam is a natural entry point for students who want flight dynamics and control fundamentals before moving into the learning-based projects.
